Story tracker — China offshore-trust income tax

MOF + State Taxation Administration Announcement 2026 No. 21 (24 July 2026) · living roundup of commentary · last updated 26 July 2026

What happened. On 24 July 2026 China's Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration jointly issued Announcement 2026 No. 21, setting out how individual income tax applies to Chinese resident individuals connected to offshore trusts. A flat 20% IIT applies to gains on assets transferred into the trust, to trust income during its life, and to income of controlled offshore entities held by the trust — taxed whether or not distributed. A ~90-day (three-month) voluntary-disclosure grace period waives late-payment surcharges (covering unpaid tax on trusts established 1 Jan 2023–31 Dec 2025 and pre-2026 trust income), and establishment-stage tax is exempted for trusts running three or more years. Framed as enforcement of existing law, not new legislation.

  • Official MOF / State Taxation Administration, via Xinhua (24 Jul). Official-line report confirming the 20% treatment across establishment / operation / termination, the three-month penalty-waived window, and retroactive-exemption relief for three-year-old trusts. Xinhua

Genuine firm commentary: pending

As at 26 July, no dedicated law/tax-firm client alert on Announcement 2026 No. 21 had been published or indexed — the big-4 China alert libraries (checked directly) show nothing yet on this topic, and substantive firm analysis at 48 hours is unusual. Voices to watch for the first formal notes: Zhong Lun (Clifford Ng has commented on the broader crackdown in earlier press), Han Kun, Withers, Baker McKenzie, and the big-4 China desks — with the key private-client questions being use of the 90-day disclosure window, settlor/beneficiary status, the three-year threshold, and the Hong Kong / Singapore knock-on for mainland families. A follow-up sweep in early-to-mid August should capture these.
