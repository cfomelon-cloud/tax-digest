Story tracker — China offshore-trust income tax

MOF + State Taxation Administration Announcement 2026 No. 21 (24 July 2026) · living roundup of commentary · last updated 27 July 2026

What happened. On 24 July 2026 China's Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration jointly issued Announcement 2026 No. 21, setting out how individual income tax applies to Chinese resident individuals connected to offshore trusts. A flat 20% IIT applies to gains on assets transferred into the trust, to trust income during its life, and to income of controlled offshore entities held by the trust — taxed whether or not distributed. A ~90-day (three-month) voluntary-disclosure grace period waives late-payment surcharges (covering unpaid tax on trusts established 1 Jan 2023–31 Dec 2025 and pre-2026 trust income), and establishment-stage tax is exempted for trusts running three or more years. Framed as enforcement of existing law, not new legislation.

Mechanics (from the primary MOF text)

  • Definition. "Offshore trust" means a trust established under foreign law, or a functionally equivalent legal arrangement.
  • Resident settlors — funding. On placing property into the trust, tax is due on the property-transfer gain (market value less original cost and reasonable expenses).
  • During the trust's life. Trust income is taxed annually whether or not distributed, at 20%, characterised as property-transfer income or as interest / dividends / bonuses by source.
  • Termination. Liquidation gains are taxed as interest / dividends / bonuses.
  • Non-residents. Report only China-source gains on funding; but distributions to resident beneficiaries trigger resident-level tax.
  • Reliefs. Foreign taxes already paid are creditable; already-taxed distributions are not taxed again; trusts running 3+ years are exempt from retroactive establishment-stage tax (lifetime income still reportable).
  • Filing. Annual filing 1 March–30 June following the tax year; unreported 2023–2025 amounts have a 90-day penalty-free disclosure window.
