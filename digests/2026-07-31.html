🇨🇳 China (Mainland) — 20% offshore-trust income tax Update / continued coverage

  • Analyst-option · professional Anli Law Firm (安理律师事务所): "Ten questions on the offshore-trust IIT regime — re-examining Announcement No. 21 (Part 1)" (29 Jul). The first substantively critical practitioner analysis (as opposed to the descriptive big-4 / law-firm alerts carried earlier in the week), setting out where Announcement 2026 No. 21 is unclear and likely to be contested. Part 1 works through five of ten issues: (1) scope — the rules are written for trusts established under foreign law, leaving open how PRC-law trusts, charitable trusts and pooled fund products are treated; (2) the residence test — reliance on "obtained foreign status" plus "primary economic interests" is criticised as vague ("主要经济利益是一个非常模糊的概念"), a real problem for foreign-passport holders with China ties; (3) the novel "装入" (placing-in) concept, which broadens the establishment-stage taxable event on a substance-over-form basis; (4) look-through taxation of beneficiaries, which the firm warns creates economic double-taxation risk without a clear foreign-tax-credit mechanism for some cross-border chains, and does not allow interest/dividend income to be offset against capital-gains — they are taxed by category; and (5) retroactivity — a three-year look-back on establishment that can extend to five years for large amounts under the general Tax Collection and Administration Law. The firm's read is that compliance and structural adjustment, not restructuring in haste, are the right near-term response. Part 2 (the remaining five issues) is flagged to follow. 安理律师事务所 (Chinese)
    Adviser read. This is the piece to read alongside the descriptive alerts, because it maps the interpretive risk rather than restating the mechanics. Three points to carry into client conversations: the residence test ("primary economic interests") is soft enough that foreign-passport settlors and protectors with continuing China ties cannot assume they are out of scope; look-through taxation plus category-by-category treatment (no netting of income against gains) can raise the effective charge above a naïve 20%-of-net estimate; and the three-to-five-year look-back means the establishment-stage exposure is wider than the headline 2023 start date suggests for larger structures. None of this changes the 22 October 2026 back-filing deadline — it sharpens the questions to resolve before filing. Treat the open issues as positions to document and support, not settled law. Full mechanics and the growing commentary set sit on the China offshore-trust tracker.
  • Reported · press 国际金融报 (International Finance News): "20% rate applies! How far does the offshore-trust IIT policy reach?" (适用20%税率！离岸信托个税新政影响几何？) (30 Jul). The first mainstream financial-press treatment in this refresh window (People's Daily group; carried via Sina Finance), blending news with named-analyst commentary. It reiterates that the 20% charge bites across all four stages — establishment, operation, distribution and termination — and frames the policy's purpose as systematically closing the classic deferral play of "book the gain, never distribute, never pay" (「只留账面收益、不作实际分配、长期不纳税」). Analysts quoted read the regime as materially eroding the tax advantage of offshore-trust structuring and expect some wealth to migrate toward domestic family trusts and other compliant onshore channels; the retroactive reach to 1 January 2023 and the back-filing window are restated. 国际金融报 (Chinese, via Sina)
  • Analyst-option · professional Lantai Law Firm (兰台律师事务所): "Interpreting the offshore-trust IIT rules — the compliance boundary for HNW cross-border wealth arrangements" (离岸信托个税新规解读：高净值客户跨境财富安排的合规边界) (29 Jul). A further practitioner note in the window, pitched squarely at HNW clients and their advisers. Its distinctive emphasis is on status change as a taxable trigger: moving from PRC-resident to non-resident does not extinguish prior obligations, and identity/status changes can themselves crystallise a settlement event — a point advisers weighing expatriation timing should not miss. It also restates the establishment-stage 20% charge on built-in gains treated as a property transfer, the annual report-and-pay obligation on trust income even absent distribution, and that the tax obligation stands independently of the trust's legal separateness under foreign law. 兰台律师事务所 (Chinese)
    Adviser read. The Lantai note sharpens one planning point the descriptive alerts underplay: expatriation is not a clean exit. For clients contemplating a change of tax residence to sidestep the regime, the change of status can itself be the event that triggers settlement, and it does nothing for exposure already accrued from 2023 onward. Sequence any residence planning against the establishment-stage and look-back exposure before treating relocation as a fix.
