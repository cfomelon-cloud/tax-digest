🇨🇳 China (Mainland) — 20% offshore-trust income tax Update / continued coverage

  • Confirmed · professional Morgan Lewis: "China Establishes New Individual Income Tax Rules for Offshore Trusts" (28 Jul). Detailed law-firm analysis of Announcements No.21 and No.15 of 2026. Key mechanics: property transferred into an offshore trust is a deemed disposition, with 20% individual income tax on the difference between market value at contribution and original cost (i.e. on the gain, not the gross value). The charge runs across the lifecycle — establishment (deemed disposition), continuation (annual income attribution) and termination (liquidation gains). Scope is broad: resident individuals funding offshore trusts, non-residents transferring China-source property, and trusts where residents keep actual control despite a nominal non-resident settlor. Offshore trustees must provide tax-category accounting, assist with filings and supply Chinese translations. Compliance clock: file and pay historical liabilities by 22 October 2026 (90 days from 24 July) to avoid late-payment surcharges, covering contributions made 1 Jan 2023 – 31 Dec 2025. Morgan Lewis
  • Confirmed · professional KPMG China — Tax Alert 05: "Tax transparency and compliance now the norm" (29 Jul). Big-4 alert covering the same two 24 July announcements. Emphasises full-lifecycle taxation (assets entering the trust, ongoing operation, distributions); application to resident individuals (including foreign nationals deriving economic benefit from China) and to non-residents transferring assets in; a foreign-tax-credit mechanism (foreign taxes paid may be credited against Chinese IIT); and anti-avoidance reach — authorities can attribute benefits distributed to residents or related parties as taxable income and may extend the look-back period where amounts are substantial. Confirms the 90-day grace window from 24 July for outstanding liabilities. KPMG China
  • Reported · trade press WealthManagement.com (Informa): the US double-tax trap (29 Jul). Estate-planning trade coverage foregrounding the cross-border exposure. The 20% charge reaches built-in gains on funding, annual trust income, unrealised appreciation on distributions, and distributions from non-resident-funded trusts. Crucially for dual-status families, it can apply to US citizens or green-card holders whose "primary economic interests are derived from within China." Worked example: transferring ~$15m of appreciated assets triggers ~$3m of Chinese tax plus separate US tax on the same gain, with no current credit mechanism to relieve the overlap. Reporting deadline flagged as 22 October 2026. WealthManagement.com
