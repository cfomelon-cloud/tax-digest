Top move. Two UK signals harden the outlook: Burnham walks back any personal-allowance unfreeze (the freeze stays), and analysts put the government's unfunded pledges near £22bn — making autumn revenue-raisers look near-certain.

Developments

🇬🇧 United Kingdom Reported

  • Reported Burnham walks back the personal-allowance unfreeze. After appearing to hint the frozen £12,570 income-tax allowance could rise, Burnham rowed back, with No.10 declining to commit to unfreezing thresholds. The freeze (running to April 2028) — a major fiscal-drag revenue-raiser — is likely to stay.
    Sources: Professional Adviser · Bloomberg
  • Reported ~£22bn of unfunded pledges → autumn tax rises 'guaranteed'. Analysis identified roughly £22bn of Burnham-government commitments not in official forecasts (energy VAT cut, extra defence, other pledges). With the manifesto ring-fencing income tax, VAT and employee NIC, commentators flagged pension tax relief, CGT and IHT reliefs as likely targets — nothing announced.
    Sources: CityAM
Adviser read: Plan on the freeze holding; watch the £100k–£125,140 personal-allowance taper and continued drag into higher bands.
Adviser read: The revenue will most plausibly come from CGT, IHT reliefs, property and fiscal drag. The pre-Budget window favours acting early on CGT disposals and large gifts.

🇸🇬 Singapore / Hong Kong Reported

  • Reported AIMA warns MAS that managers eye a Hong Kong move. The Alternative Investment Management Association told MAS that hedge-fund and asset managers are weighing relocation to Hong Kong, citing HK's proposed carried-interest break and pointing to individual taxation as 'the decisive lever' for mobile talent — urging lower taxes and headcount-linked relief. Industry submission, not a tax change.
    Sources: Yahoo Finance (carrying Bloomberg)
Adviser read: Reinforces the HK vs Singapore competitive dynamic for family offices and principals — a factor in siting decisions, though no rule has changed.
