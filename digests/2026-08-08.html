China (Mainland) / Hong Kong — offshore-insurance income tax

🇨🇳🇭🇰 Update / continued coverage Beijing's tax authority calls the insurance levy old law, not a new tax

  • Confirmed · official / state media State Taxation Administration response — "not a new policy, not aimed at Hong Kong" (7 Aug). Two days after Caixin's exclusive that Beijing and Hangzhou bureaux had begun charging 20% IIT on mainland residents' Hong Kong / offshore policy returns, the STA gave an on-the-record response carried by Xinhua: taxing residents' overseas insurance returns is not newly introduced and not targeted at Hong Kong's insurance market. Its logic: China's IIT law has always taxed tax residents on worldwide income; investment returns are treated the same whatever the asset (insurance, securities, funds) and whatever the country or region of origin; and enforcement is simply catching up now that CRS information exchange has matured. The STA framed the drive as anti-avoidance and equitable-taxation, and urged the market not to "over-interpret." For advisers this is the clearest signal yet that Beijing intends to hold the line on the substance while damping the share-price panic. Xinhua 新華網 (Chinese) · i-CABLE 有線 (Chinese)
  • Reputable press SCMP — "Beijing says 20% offshore insurance tax not new — and does not target Hong Kong" (7 Aug). The South China Morning Post carries the STA statement for an international audience, tying it to the sharp falls in AIA, Prudential and HSBC earlier in the week and stressing the official line that mainland residents must report all global investment earnings — "dividends and other returns arising from their overseas insurance policies" — regardless of jurisdiction, and that the measure reflects international tax-compliance standards rather than a Hong Kong-specific attack. South China Morning Post (subscription)
  • Reputable press The Standard / Dim Sum Daily — market-jitters framing (7 Aug). Hong Kong English outlets corroborate the same clarification: offshore insurance income is subject to domestic IIT for Chinese tax residents — "all income must be declared and taxed according to law" — with the STA statement issued expressly to calm the volatility that hit HSBC, Standard Chartered and Prudential in London and Hong Kong trading. Useful accessible English corroboration of the official wording. The Standard · Dim Sum Daily
  • Explainer Securities Times (证券时报/stcn) — what is actually taxed (6 Aug). A helpful mechanics explainer for client conversations. The charge bites on the investment return element of savings / participating / investment-linked policies — dividend and participating distributions, surrenders, policy reductions and account-value withdrawals — taxed at 20% as interest/dividend/investment income. Pure protection payouts are outside the net: death benefits, critical-illness claims and medical reimbursements are exempt under the IIT law. Worked example: pay RMB1m of premium, receive RMB1.3m back, and the RMB300k gain is potentially taxable. It is the CRS data flow — not any new rule — that makes this enforceable now. 证券时报 Securities Times (Chinese)
Adviser read. Take two messages to insurance-holding mainland clients. First, on substance: this is not going away — the STA has publicly planted its flag on worldwide taxation of policy returns, so the planning question is scope and timing, not whether the charge exists. Second, on scope: the tax is on the growth/return leg (participating dividends, ILAS account gains, interest on prepaid premiums, surrender gains), not on protection payouts, so pure life-cover and CI policies are largely unaffected — separate the two when reviewing a book. Practical steps: identify which mainland-resident clients hold savings/participating/ILAS contracts, gather premium-cost and distribution records to compute the taxable gain, and read the insurance thread alongside the offshore-trust regime (same 20% rate, same CRS enabler, different asset class) whose disclosure window still closes ~21 Oct. Expect big-4 and specialist HK/PRC firm alerts to firm up the calculation mechanics over the coming days.
