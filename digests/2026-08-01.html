China (Mainland) — offshore-trust income tax

🇨🇳 Update / continued coverage — new practitioner analysis

  • Firm analysis AllBright Law Offices (锦天城) — "Nine confusions, two scenarios, one bottom line": a practical Q&A on Announcement No. 21 (offshore-trust tax research series, part 2), 30 July. A leading PRC firm sets out the questions clients are actually asking now that Announcement 2026 No. 21 is live, and its answers sharpen several planning points beyond the official Q&A. It stresses that a foreign passport does not break Chinese tax residence where the individual's "primary economic interests" remain in China, so overseas citizenship alone will not take a settlor outside the 20% charge; that nominee arrangements are ignored — the actual contributor of assets is taxed regardless of nominal ownership (substance over form); that a Chinese resident who controls a trust established by a non-resident is taxed as if he had contributed the assets himself; and that the three taxable events — asset contribution, annual trust income (taxed whether or not distributed), and termination — are each computed independently, with no offsetting and with trust-level management fees and expenses non-deductible, which can push the effective burden well above a headline 20%. On timing it reads the 90-day settlement window as closing on or about 21 October 2026, waiving late-payment surcharges (滞纳金) on historical catch-up filings, and frames the core decision — unwind the structure now or maintain it — as turning on whether the trust's original purpose was tax deferral (now largely defeated) or genuine asset protection and succession (which may still justify keeping it). AllBright Law Offices 锦天城 (Chinese)
Adviser read. This is the most useful practitioner note yet on the "what do I actually do" questions, and it is more cautionary than the official interpretation: the foreign-passport, nominee and non-resident-settlor points each close an escape route clients may assume is open, and the no-offset / no-deduction mechanics mean the real cost of a legacy structure is often materially higher than 20% of a single gain. Near-term: for any client with a Chinese-resident nexus behind an offshore trust, diarise the ~21 October disclosure deadline, assemble establishment-date and cost-base evidence, and pressure-test the "keep or unwind" question against the trust's original purpose — but continue to hold off on irreversible restructuring until the big-4 and specialist HK/PRC alerts converge. Secondary jurisdictions

🇹🇼 Taiwan — Estate & Gift Tax Act amendment (anti-disclaimer / "first-wife" clause)

  • Reported Executive Yuan approves draft Estate and Gift Tax Act amendment closing the disclaimer-of-inheritance loophole (30 July; now heads to the Legislative Yuan). Taiwan's Cabinet cleared a package that changes who bears estate tax when wealth is moved before death. Under the current rule an heir can disclaim the inheritance (拋棄繼承) and shunt the whole estate-tax bill onto the remaining beneficiaries; the amendment makes each recipient of "deemed estate" property bear estate tax in proportion to what they received, so a disclaiming heir who took large lifetime gifts can no longer dump the liability on others. Mechanically it keeps the two-year look-back (gifts to specified relatives within two years of death are pulled back into the gross estate as "擬制遺產"/deemed estate) and adds a spousal "大老婆 (first-wife) clause": gifts to a spouse within two years of death are deemed estate on which the recipient spouse is liable, while the spouse's statutory surplus-property division claim (Civil Code art. 1030-1) is preserved. The Finance Ministry illustrated it with a deemed-estate tax of NT$400,000 payable by the recipient spouse, and the bill also removes the monetary cap on installment payments. The reform follows a 2024 Supreme Court case in which heirs who had received ~NT$3 billion in stock options disclaimed the inheritance to avoid an ~NT$57 million liability that would have fallen on another beneficiary. [Reported — Cabinet-approved draft; not yet enacted.] Taipei Times, 31 Jul (English) · 中央社 CNA, 30 Jul (Chinese) · 聯合新聞網 UDN, 30 Jul (Chinese)
Adviser read. For Taiwan-connected families this reopens the pre-death gifting playbook. The two-year deemed-estate window is unchanged, but the shift to proportional, recipient-borne liability neutralises the "disclaim-and-shift" tactic and the spouse clause closes the route of routing gifts through a spouse to dilute the taxable estate. It is still only a Cabinet draft — watch the Legislative Yuan passage and, critically, any retroactive/transitional application (the Chinese coverage flags a 溯及適用 provision), which would matter for gifts already made. Near-term: for clients weighing large intra-family transfers, factor in that the intended recipient — not the residual heirs — will carry the proportional estate tax if death falls within two years.

🇹🇼 台灣——《遺產及贈與稅法》修正（防拋棄繼承／「大老婆條款」）

  • 已披露 行政院通過《遺產及贈與稅法》修正草案，堵塞拋棄繼承漏洞（7月30日；後續送立法院審議）。台灣行政院拍板之修法方案，改變了於生前移轉財產時「由誰負擔遺產稅」。依現行規定，繼承人可拋棄繼承而將全部遺產稅負轉嫁予其他受益人；修法後，取得「擬制遺產」財產之各受贈人須依其受贈財產占遺產總額之比例分擔遺產稅，故已受領大額生前贈與而拋棄繼承者，將無法再把稅負推給他人。機制上維持死亡前二年之回溯（死亡前二年內對特定親屬之贈與併入遺產總額課稅，即「擬制遺產」），並新增配偶「大老婆條款」：死亡前二年內對配偶之贈與列為擬制遺產、由受贈配偶負擔，而配偶依民法第1030-1條之剩餘財產分配請求權仍予保留。財政部以擬制遺產之遺產稅40萬元、由受贈配偶繳納為例說明；草案並取消分期繳納之金額門檻。此次修法源於2024年一件最高法院案例：繼承人於受領約新台幣30億元股票選擇權後拋棄繼承，以規避約新台幣5,700萬元、原將落在另一受益人身上之稅負。〔已披露——行政院通過之草案，尚未完成立法。〕台北時報，7月31日（英文） · 中央社 CNA，7月30日（中文） · 聯合新聞網 UDN，7月30日（中文）
顧問解讀。對具台灣連結之家族而言，此舉重新界定了生前贈與的規劃空間。二年擬制遺產之窗口不變，惟改為依比例、由受贈人負擔之稅負，已使「拋棄並轉嫁」之操作失效，而配偶條款亦封堵了藉配偶轉贈以稀釋應稅遺產之途徑。目前仍僅為行政院草案——應關注立法院之審議進度，尤其是任何溯及／過渡適用條款（中文報導提及「溯及適用」），此對已完成之贈與影響甚鉅。近期建議：客戶如考量大額家族內部移轉，須將此納入考量——若於二年內身故，承擔比例遺產稅者將為原定受贈人，而非其餘繼承人。
