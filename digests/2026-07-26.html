Top cross-border move. China has issued detailed rules bringing offshore trusts into the individual income tax net — resident settlors/beneficiaries face a 20% charge on trust income, with a three-month penalty-waiver disclosure window. It's the most significant private-client development of the last 48 hours and directly affects Chinese HNW families using offshore trust structures (and their advisers in Hong Kong and Singapore). Elsewhere the priority jurisdictions have been quiet in-window.

Priority jurisdictions

🇨🇳 China (Mainland) Material development

  • Confirmed Offshore trusts brought into IIT (24 July 2026). The Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration jointly issued an announcement clarifying that Chinese resident individuals must declare and pay individual income tax on income from transferring assets into offshore trusts and on income generated during the life of such trusts. Asset-transfer gains are taxed as "income from transfer of property"; lifetime income is taxed at 20% (as property-transfer income or as "interest, dividends and bonuses," depending on the return). A three-year operational threshold exempts establishment-stage tax for trusts running over three years (lifetime income still taxable), and a three-month voluntary-disclosure window offers penalty waivers for late payment. Framed as enforcement of existing law, not new legislation. Xinhua · Caproasia · Bloomberg via Yahoo
Adviser read: this closes a core succession-planning route for mainland HNW families and follows the running CRS/offshore-income enforcement drive. Affected clients should use the three-month disclosure window rather than wait for an STA notice, and revisit any offshore-trust structure (settlor status, distribution timing, and whether the three-year threshold applies). Advisers in Hong Kong and Singapore serving mainland-resident families should expect restructuring queries and heightened source-of-wealth scrutiny.

Firm & professional commentary: Early market analysis frames the announcement as a move to "look-through taxation" of cross-border wealth structures (Yuan Talks, 24 Jul). No dedicated big-4 / private-client law-firm client alert has published within the last 48h yet; alerts from firms such as Withers, KPMG, Baker McKenzie and Zhong Lun typically follow within a few days and will be captured in a later edition as they appear.
