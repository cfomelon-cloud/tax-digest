Top cross-border moves

🌐 China's offshore-trust tax — the wealth-management fallout reaches mainstream press

  • Reported · top-tier press CNBC — "China's super-rich 'in shock' and hunting for cash as Beijing issues surprise tax on offshore trusts" (5 Aug). The first major Western-press feature on the behavioural impact of Announcement 2026 No. 21 (effective 24 July), which brings a Chinese tax resident's offshore-trust income within a flat 20% individual income tax at three points — settlement of assets into the trust, annual income during its life whether or not distributed, and liquidation. CNBC reports that private banks, trustees and law firms are fielding a surge of urgent enquiries from ultra-high-net-worth families, many of whom had assumed offshore trusts placed assets beyond China's reach; the practical squeeze is liquidity, because the charge can fall on unrealised or undistributed gains while the underlying wealth sits in illiquid holdings (private company stock, pre-IPO stakes, real estate). The article frames the 90-day voluntary-disclosure window — running from 24 July to roughly 21 October 2026 — as the near-term pressure point driving the scramble for advice, with CRS information exchange leaving little room to wait it out. CNBC
Adviser read. This is continued coverage, not a new rule — but the CNBC piece is the clearest sign yet that the story has moved from technical alert to client action. The recurring pain point advisers should pre-empt is funding the charge: where a Chinese-resident settlor or beneficiary sits behind an illiquid offshore structure, the 20% may be due before any cash is distributed. Near-term steps unchanged from last week: identify the Chinese-resident nexus across each structure, diarise the ~21 October disclosure deadline, assemble establishment-date and cost-base records, and model the cash cost of settlement — but resist forced asset sales or hasty restructuring driven by headline panic rather than a structure-specific analysis.
