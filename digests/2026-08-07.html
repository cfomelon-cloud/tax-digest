Adviser read. The regulators' framing matters for how you counsel clients: officials are calling this enforcement of an existing rule, not a new levy — which cuts both ways. It lowers the odds of a sudden, formalised back-tax campaign, but it removes any "we didn't know it applied" comfort: a mainland tax resident holding a HK savings, participating, universal-life or ILAS policy has, on this reading, always owed 20% on the policy's investment return (dividends/bonuses distributed and interest credited on prepaid premiums), computed separately from onshore income, with foreign-tax credits and no aggregation. Practical near-term steps mirror the trust playbook: identify which policyholders are Chinese tax residents; distinguish taxable returns from the (non-taxable) premium and death benefit; assemble records of distributions and prepaid-premium interest; and weigh whether to surface historic returns proactively where a local bureau has already opened cases. Enforcement remains case-by-case with no national announcement — advise clients against panic surrenders while the position formalises.
