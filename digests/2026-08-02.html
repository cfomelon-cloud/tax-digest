Top cross-border move

Taiwan's succession-law overhaul — estate tax and forced heirship reformed on parallel tracks. For Taiwan-connected HNW families the planning landscape is shifting on two fronts within a fortnight. On the tax side, the Cabinet's draft Estate & Gift Tax Act amendment reallocates who bears estate tax on pre-death gifts, neutralising the "disclaim-and-shift" tactic and adding a spousal fix. On the civil-law side, the Legislature has stripped siblings of their guaranteed reserved portion, widening testamentary freedom for the childless and for those wishing to benefit a spouse, charity or non-family heir. Both point the same way — toward wills that actually control the outcome — and both reward reviewing Taiwan-exposed estate plans now, while confirming effective dates and any transitional/retroactive provisions.

Taiwan — succession & estate tax reform

🇹🇼 Update / continued coverage — Estate & Gift Tax Act amendment (anti-disclaimer / spousal clause)

  • Reported Focus Taiwan — "Cabinet proposes estate tax changes to fix unfair pre-death gift rules" (31 July). Fresh in-window coverage of the Executive Yuan draft carried in yesterday's edition, adding mechanics beyond the earlier reports. It confirms the core change: recipients of gifts made within two years of the giver's death must bear estate tax on those assets in proportion to their share — even if they disclaim the inheritance, with any remaining liability split among the heirs, so a disclaiming heir can no longer dump the whole bill on others. Newly detailed here is the spousal deduction fix: qualifying pre-death gifts will now count toward the spousal deduction, lowering the surviving spouse's burden rather than inflating it. Two worked examples illustrate the mischief being cured — a wife who received NT$300m+ in stock under a year before death, after which a 6-year-old heir was left an NT$57m bill via a disclaimer; and a Taoyuan wife gifted NT$130m of land under two weeks before death who faced an NT$14.6m estate-tax bill. The bill has been referred to the Legislature; no effective date is set. Focus Taiwan (31 Jul)
Adviser read. This is the clearest English-language account yet of the two moving parts: the switch to proportional, recipient-borne liability (which kills disclaim-and-shift) and the taxpayer-favourable spousal-deduction change (which softens the "first-wife" deemed-estate charge flagged in earlier coverage). For clients weighing large intra-family transfers, the planning point is unchanged from yesterday — assume the intended recipient, not the residual heirs, will carry the proportional estate tax if death falls within two years — but the spousal-deduction detail is worth modelling where a spouse is the intended donee. Still a Cabinet draft: watch Legislative Yuan passage and any transitional/retroactive application. [Reported — Cabinet-approved draft; not yet enacted.]

🇹🇼 New thread — Civil Code forced-heirship reform (siblings' reserved portion abolished)

  • Commentary Taipei Times editorial — "Protecting will-making autonomy" (2 August). The fresh in-window item: an op-ed (by a former land-administration official) welcoming the Legislative Yuan's recent abolition of siblings' forced-heirship reserved portion under Civil Code art. 1223, while arguing the reform must be paired with stronger safeguards. The amendment removes the mandatory minimum share (previously one-third of a sibling's statutory portion) that siblings could claim against a will; siblings retain their intestate succession rights (art. 1138 unchanged) where there is no valid will. The author supports the change as reducing inheritance litigation and honouring the deceased's wishes, but calls for improved will notarisation, guardianship and adult-trust mechanisms to protect elderly or cognitively-impaired testators from undue influence — the flip-side risk of greater testamentary freedom. Taipei Times editorial (2 Aug)
  • Confirmed · background The underlying reform, for context. The Legislature passed the art. 1223 amendment in late July; it takes effect six months after presidential promulgation, and abolishes only the forced-heirship protection for siblings, not their statutory (intestate) inheritance rights. Background and firm analysis: Focus Taiwan (28 Jul) · Taipei Times (29 Jul) · Celsum Attorneys explainer (28 Jul).
Adviser read. For Taiwan-connected families this materially widens testamentary freedom and rewards a will review now — most obviously for childless couples (who can now direct the whole estate to a surviving spouse without a sibling claw-back) and for clients wishing to benefit a charity or non-family heir over siblings. The practical corollary the editorial and firm commentary both stress: freedom only bites through a valid, carefully drafted and internationally-coordinated will — absent one, statutory succession still applies and siblings can inherit on intestacy. Two dates to fix: confirm the presidential promulgation date to set the six-month effective clock for the Civil Code change, and track the separate Legislative Yuan passage of the Estate & Gift Tax Act amendment above. The two reforms run on independent tracks and should be modelled together.

台灣 — 繼承與遺產稅改革

🇹🇼 更新／後續報導 — 《遺產及贈與稅法》修正（反拋棄／配偶條款）

  • 已披露 中央社Focus Taiwan — 〈內閣提出遺產稅修正以矯正死亡前贈與之不公規則〉（7月31日）。對昨日日報所載行政院草案於期間內之新報導，補充先前報導以外之機制。其確認核心變動：於贈與人死亡前兩年內受贈財產者，須就該等財產按其應繼份比例負擔遺產稅——即使其拋棄繼承亦然，剩餘稅負則由各繼承人分擔，如此拋棄繼承之人不再能將全部稅單轉嫁他人。本篇新增揭露配偶扣除額之調整：符合條件之死亡前贈與現將計入配偶扣除額，降低而非墊高生存配偶之負擔。兩則實例說明所欲矯正之弊端——一名妻子於死亡前不到一年受贈逾新台幣3億元股票，其後一名6歲繼承人因拋棄繼承而背負新台幣5,700萬元稅單；另一名桃園妻子於死亡前不到兩週受贈新台幣1.3億元土地，面臨新台幣1,460萬元遺產稅單。該法案已送立法院審議；尚未定生效日期。Focus Taiwan（7月31日）
顧問解讀。此為迄今對兩項變動最清晰之英文說明：改採按比例、由受贈人負擔之稅負（終結拋棄轉嫁），以及有利納稅人之配偶扣除額調整（緩和先前報導所指之「大老婆」擬制遺產課稅）。就衡量大額家族內移轉之客戶而言，規劃要點與昨日相同——若死亡發生於兩年內，應假設由預定受贈人（而非剩餘繼承人）承擔按比例之遺產稅——惟於配偶為預定受贈對象時，配偶扣除額之細節值得試算。仍屬內閣草案：留意立法院之通過及任何過渡／溯及適用。〔已披露——內閣核定草案，尚未立法。〕

🇹🇼 新議題 — 《民法》特留分改革（廢除兄弟姊妹特留分）

  • 評論 《台北時報》社論 — 〈保障遺囑自主〉（8月2日）。本期間內之新項目：一篇社論（作者為前地政官員）肯定立法院近日廢除《民法》第1223條下兄弟姊妹之特留分強制繼承保障，同時主張改革須輔以更完善之保障機制。該修正刪除兄弟姊妹得對遺囑主張之強制最低份額（原為其應繼分之三分之一）；於無有效遺囑時，兄弟姊妹仍保有法定（無遺囑）繼承權（第1138條未變）。作者支持此變動可減少繼承訴訟並尊重逝者意願，但呼籲強化遺囑公證、監護及成年信託機制，以保護年長或認知受損之立遺囑人免受不當影響——此為遺囑自由擴大之另一面風險。《台北時報》社論（8月2日）
  • 已確認 · 背景 相關改革背景。立法院於7月下旬三讀通過第1223條修正；其自總統公布後六個月施行，且僅廢除兄弟姊妹之特留分保障，並未廢除其法定（無遺囑）繼承權。背景與事務所分析：Focus Taiwan（7月28日） · 《台北時報》（7月29日） · 世勤律師事務所解說（7月28日）。
顧問解讀。對涉台家族而言，此舉實質擴大遺囑自由，並使現在檢視遺囑更具價值——最明顯者為無子女之夫妻（現可將全部遺產留予生存配偶，而無兄弟姊妹之追索），以及欲將財產優先留予慈善機構或非家族繼承人而非兄弟姊妹之客戶。社論與事務所評論皆強調之實務推論：此等自由唯有透過有效、審慎草擬並跨境協調之遺囑方能實現——如無遺囑，仍適用法定繼承，兄弟姊妹得依無遺囑繼承取得財產。應確認之兩項日期：查明總統公布日以確定民法修正之六個月施行起算，並追蹤上述《遺產及贈與稅法》修正於立法院之通過。兩項改革各行其軌，宜一併試算。
