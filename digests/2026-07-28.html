China (Mainland) — offshore-trust income tax

🇨🇳 The rules, the official interpretation, and the first firm analysis

  • Confirmed · official MOF / State Taxation Administration press Q&A ("答记者问"). The two ministries published their official policy-interpretation note alongside Announcement 2026 No. 21. It confirms that a Chinese tax resident is taxed at the flat 20% rate at three points — when property is settled into an offshore trust (on the transfer gain: market value less original cost and reasonable expenses), annually on trust income during the trust's life whether or not distributed, and on liquidation — and, crucially for planning, it spells out the protections: foreign taxes already paid are creditable against the Chinese charge; income already taxed on distribution is not taxed twice; trusts that have run for three or more years are exempt from retroactive establishment-stage tax (though lifetime income stays reportable); and there is a 90-day voluntary-disclosure window (from 24 July) to regularise unreported 2023–2025 amounts without late-payment surcharges. It frames the measure as clarifying how existing IIT law applies to offshore trusts — enabled by matured CRS exchange — not as a new tax. The most useful primary reference to hand a client. mof.gov.cn 政策解读 (Chinese)
  • Reputable press Caixin — "China issues detailed 20% tax guidance on offshore trusts" (25 Jul). China's most respected independent financial outlet confirms the 20% charge at establishment, operation and liquidation, reiterates the 90-day settlement window and the look-back to trusts established from 2023 and pre-2026 income, and places the move within Beijing's two-year drive to tax residents' overseas income. It notes the target is "low-transparency jurisdictions" used to conceal assets; fuller detail sits behind Caixin's paywall. Caixin Global (subscription)
  • State media Continued official coverage. Further restatements of the clarification, useful for corroboration and Chinese-language sourcing: China.org.cn (27 Jul), China Economic Net (25 Jul), and the original Xinhua explainer (24 Jul).
  • Firm alert Dentons — "why it matters" for advisers and HNW families (28 Jul). The first substantive private-client firm write-up since the rules took effect. Dentons reads the measure as materially wider than a China-only rule: wherever a Chinese tax resident is a settlor, beneficiary or controlling party of an offshore trust — very often held through Hong Kong, BVI or Cayman — trust income and gains can be attributed and taxed at 20%, backed by the 90-day disclosure expectation and CRS-driven information exchange. Practical steps it flags: review the residency and roles of settlors, protectors and beneficiaries; map which structures now have a Chinese-resident nexus; and revisit distribution timing and governance before enforcement intensifies. 中國（大陸）— 離岸信託個人所得稅
